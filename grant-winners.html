Q2 2026

Sarupa Roy

Congratulations to Dr. Sarupa Roy of the ’t Hart Lab at the University of Münster, our Q2 2026 Graduate Research Grant recipient!

About Sarupa

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow in the laboratory of Prof. Peter ’t Hart at the Institute of Biochemistry, University of Münster, Germany. My current research focuses on developing oligonucleotide–peptide conjugates to regulate alternative pre-mRNA splicing by targeting RNA-binding proteins. I use chemical biology approaches to design molecular tools for understanding and manipulating RNA processing pathways.

I completed my PhD at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Pune, India, where my research focused on the chemical biology of nucleic acids, particularly the study of nucleic acid secondary structures.

Q1 2026

Kathrin Weller

Congratulations to Kathrin Weller of the Mulder Lab at Leiden University Medical Center, our Q1 2026 Graduate Research Grant recipient!

About Kathrin

I am a third-year PhD candidate in Dr. Monique Mulder’s laboratory at the LUMC in Leiden, the Netherlands, where my research focuses on the role of the ubiquitin–proteasome system in Huntington’s disease. My work centers on the use of peptide-based tools and chemical biology approaches to investigate disease mechanisms and identify potential therapeutic targets.

Using engineered ubiquitin probes and disease-relevant cell models, I study changes in protein quality control pathways associated with mutant huntingtin. My research aims to contribute to a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying Huntington’s disease and to support the development of novel therapeutic strategies.

Q4 2025

Sebin Abraham

Congratulations to Sebin Abraham, our Q4 2025 CSBio Graduate Research Grant recipient! We’re excited to support his work and look forward to following his research journey as it continues.

About Sebin

I am a fourth year PhD student in Dr. Wenshe Liu’s laboratory at Texas A&M University, where my research focuses on engineering phage display platforms using unnatural amino acids to discover next-generation peptide ligands for clinically relevant and previously undruggable targets. I began my research career as an undergraduate student at IISER Bhopal, where I developed a strong foundation in chemical biology and translational research, before moving to the United States to pursue my doctoral training at TAMU.

My research focuses on targeting protein–protein interactions using synthetic biology derived peptide libraries and structure-guided covalent macrocyclic peptide design. My research achievements include multiple awards including A.E. Martell Graduate Student Excellence Award, invited oral and poster presentations at national and international forums and a recent nomination to the Global Young Scientists Summit (GYSS), Singapore.
